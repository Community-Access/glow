# Accessibility Agents: Building Human-Centered AI Workflows for Trusted Accessibility Automation at Scale

A full-day, hands-on workshop at Accessing Higher Ground 2026. Monday 16
November, 10:30 AM to 4:30 PM Mountain Time, Matchless, Hilton Denver City
Center. Bring a Windows or Mac laptop, and headphones if you use a screen
reader. Beginner level.

## The problem

Higher education accessibility teams face WCAG 2.2 and the new Title II
requirements with the same small staff as before. Every syllabus, slide deck,
scanned reading and course page is in scope, and most of them are documents,
not web pages. Nobody fixes that by working harder. The question is how to
scale without losing quality, accountability or transparency.

## What you will do

- Design your own accessibility agent: six plain-English answers about its
  role, its task, the standards it must cite, what it produces, and who
  reviews it. No code.
- Put it to work on a realistic sample course full of planted barriers,
  with GitHub Copilot in VS Code or with the AI assistant you already use.
- Ground it in real evidence from GLOW, axe-core and Accessibility Insights,
  so you can verify its claims and WCAG 2.2 citations.
- Build the office: add your agent to an agent team of coordinated
  specialists and run the team across the whole course.
- Share it if approved: review privacy and licensing before opening
  GitHub's form, which sends the text before submission. Otherwise keep a
  reviewed local copy with a permission follow-up. Approved contributions
  are reviewed before promotion to Accessibility Agents.
- Leave with a 30-day plan for your own campus.

The plan fits one step of an existing disability resource center workflow:
approved inputs, a draft destination, a staff reviewer and a deadline. You
will choose a small pilot and measure total staff time, quality and delivery
before expanding it. Eligibility and accommodation decisions remain with
authorized people, never the agent.

## Who it is for

Accessibility professionals, disability resource staff, alternate format
producers, instructional designers, faculty developers, IT accessibility
staff, and the people who lead them. You do not need to be a developer, and
you do not need to have used VS Code or GitHub before.

## What you need

A laptop and one campus-approved path. GitHub is needed for the VS Code
paths, GitHub Copilot or approved sharing, not for the written-example
route. The paths share learning goals, not identical tool capabilities. letitglow.app/ahg/paths compares them
and has every setup step.

1. The browser path: nothing to install. ChatGPT, Copilot on github.com,
   Claude, Gemini or your campus assistant, signed in, with one text per
   block from letitglow.app/ahg/assistant. About 10 minutes to set up.
2. VS Code on your computer: VS Code with the AHG 2026 profile, the agent
   kit from letitglow.app/ahg, and Copilot Free. About 25 minutes.
3. VS Code for the Web: vscode.dev in Chrome or Edge, with the kit opened
   from your laptop. About 15 minutes. Verify command discovery, writing
   files and reopening them; desktop feature parity is not assumed.

Not sure? Take the browser path. There is a setup table outside the room
from 10:00 on the day.

Campus approval comes first. If AI tools or public sharing are not approved,
use the written examples in Word or on paper and keep your work local.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the day you will be able to:

1. Describe an accessibility task as an agent: role, task, trusted guidance,
   output format and human review.
2. Ground an agent's answers in checker evidence and cite WCAG 2.2 criteria.
3. Name the barriers no automated checker finds, and write the human review
   step that catches them.
4. Explain how an agent team of specialists and a coordinator scales work
   across a course.
5. Contribute to an open-source project on GitHub, and see your work credited.
6. Plan one workflow to try on your own campus in the next 30 days.

## Two rules, all day

1. Never paste anything private into any AI: student records, health or
   disability information, or private identifying details. We use a sample course.
2. Agents draft. People decide. Every agent ends with a named person
   checking its work.
