## Where it fits in your office

An agent helps most inside work you already do, not beside it. Before you
try yours, answer four questions:

1. What starts it? A request, a new course shell, a term deadline, a vendor
   report.
2. What does it read? A checker report, a syllabus, a request form, with
   nothing private in it.
3. Where does its draft go? Your ticket system, your remediation log, an
   email you edit before sending.
4. Who signs off? The person named in its Human review.

Then check two things with your campus: which AI assistant is approved for
this work, and what information may go into it.

## A small pilot your center can run

1. Week 1: choose one task and up to five fictional or approved non-sensitive
   examples. Record how long the existing process takes, including review.
2. Week 2: run the agent beside that process. A staff reviewer compares each
   draft with the evidence. Nothing is sent or changed automatically.
3. Week 3: revise one instruction and repeat the same cases, including one
   with missing evidence. Asking for evidence is a successful result.
4. By day 30: compare total staff time, errors, rework and on-time delivery.
   The process owner decides whether to continue, revise or stop. A faster
   draft is not a win if review takes longer or quality falls.

Keep the agent version, evidence source and date, reviewer role, decision,
due date and unresolved barriers in your existing ticket or log. Keep student
records in the center's approved system, outside the AI and the public kit.
Use staff roles in AI prompts; assign actual staff in the approved system.

Stop the pilot if private data appears, evidence cannot be verified, or a
required review or delivery deadline would be missed. Use the existing manual
process and notify the responsible campus team. Do not delay an accommodation
while testing. Agents do not decide eligibility, accommodations or purchases.

No approved AI tool yet? Use the same eight questions and the worked examples
in Word or on paper. A workflow map and an approval request are useful first
steps; do not bypass campus policy to finish the exercise.
