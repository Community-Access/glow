Pre-event message

Subject

Accessibility Agents workshop, 16 November: choose a path, 10 to 25 minutes

Body

Hello,

You are booked into Accessibility Agents: Building Human-Centered AI Workflows, the full-day workshop at Accessing Higher Ground on Monday 16 November, 10:30 AM to 4:30 PM Mountain Time, in Matchless.

By the end of the day you will have designed your own accessibility agent, added it to a team of agents that works through a whole course, and prepared it for an approved open-source contribution or local use. You do not need to be a developer. You do not need to have used VS Code or GitHub before. Every step on the day comes with a step card and a helper.

Before the day: choose a path

There are three paths into the same day, and every one ends with your own agent and a plan. Sharing on GitHub is conditional on permission. Compare the three paths. Not sure? Take the browser path.

  • The browser path, about 10 minutes, nothing to install: use ChatGPT, Copilot on github.com, Claude, Gemini or your campus assistant, signed in. No public directory listing or connector setup is needed.
  • VS Code on your computer, about 25 minutes: install VS Code and import the AHG 2026 profile.
  • VS Code for the Web, about 15 minutes, nothing to install: the same VS Code at vscode.dev, in Chrome or Edge.

Then, on any path:

  1. If your chosen path or approved sharing needs GitHub, make a free account at github.com. Use your work email if your institution allows it.
  2. Follow your path's steps on the setup page, and run the ready check. Save and reopen its practice file before declaring setup complete.
  3. If public sharing is approved, try the practice-agent form. Opening the GitHub form sends the text before submission. Otherwise keep practice local; public sharing is not a readiness requirement.
  4. Answer the five-minute survey. It asks for your name and registration email, your role and setup, any access needs, and what you hope to work on. A GitHub username is optional. The workshop team uses your answers to prepare; they stay in GLOW, are not sent to an AI or GitHub, and will be deleted by 31 January 2027. Do not include student names, records or accommodation details.

Want the short version to pass on to a colleague? Read the plain-language workshop description.

If a step does not work, write to the workshop support team before the day, or come to the setup table outside the room from 10:00 on the day. Locked-down work laptop? Take the browser path: it needs nothing installed. Or write to us; we will sort it out together, and nobody is left out on the day.

Use only an assistant your campus permits. A browser does not bypass campus data or account rules. If no AI tool or public sharing is approved, bring the written examples and work in Word or on paper with a helper; keep your agent local until sharing is approved.

Bring

  • A Windows or Mac laptop and its charger.
  • Headphones, if you use a screen reader. Read "If you use a screen reader" in the kit README before the day.
  • One real accessibility problem from your job. Nothing private: no student names, records or accommodation details. You will work on a sample course, and your problem is what makes your agent yours.

What the day looks like

  • Morning: design your agent in plain English, and see it work on a course document with GitHub Copilot or the assistant you chose.
  • After lunch: ground it in real evidence from GLOW, axe and Accessibility Insights, so you can verify its claims and WCAG 2.2 citations.
  • Afternoon: add it to an agent team and watch the team work through the whole course, then review it for approved sharing or keep a local copy with a permission follow-up.
  • The last five minutes are the session evaluation. It shapes next year's conference.

If you need anything

Large print, a particular seat, a pause, a repeat: ask for any of it, before or during. That is not an interruption of this workshop; it is the point of it.

See you in Denver,

Jeff Bishop University of Arizona, IT Accessibility Consultant
