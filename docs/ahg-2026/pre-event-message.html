Pre-event message

Send this to registered participants by 20 October, again on 6 November, and again two days before. It is the setup checklist for the day, so it has to be short, numbered and impossible to misread.

Keep the first screen to what to do. Everything else comes after.

Subject

Accessibility Agents workshop, 16 November: choose a path, 10 to 25 minutes

Body

Hello,

You are booked into Accessibility Agents: Building Human-Centered AI Workflows, the full-day workshop at Accessing Higher Ground on Monday 16 November, 10:30 AM to 4:30 PM Mountain Time, in Matchless.

By the end of the day you will have designed your own accessibility agent, added it to a team of agents that works through a whole course, and contributed it to an open-source project with your name on it. You do not need to be a developer. You do not need to have used VS Code or GitHub before. Every step on the day comes with a step card and a helper.

Before the day: choose a path

There are three paths into the same day, and every one ends with your own agent, shared on GitHub with your name on it. letitglow.app/ahg/paths compares them. Not sure? Take the browser path.

  • The browser path, about 10 minutes, nothing to install: use ChatGPT, Copilot on github.com, Claude, Gemini or your campus assistant, signed in.
  • VS Code on your computer, about 25 minutes: install VS Code and import the AHG 2026 profile.
  • VS Code for the Web, about 15 minutes, nothing to install: the same VS Code at vscode.dev, in Chrome or Edge.

Then, on any path:

  1. Make a free GitHub account at github.com, if you do not have one. Use your work email if your institution allows it.
  2. Follow your path's steps at letitglow.app/ahg/paths, and run the ready check. It takes two minutes and tells you whether you are set.
  3. Share your practice agent at letitglow.app/ahg/share: one form, one button, and a reply within a minute. On the day you do it once more, with your real agent.
  4. Answer the five-minute survey at letitglow.app/ahg/survey, so we have your path, your laptop and any access needs ready on the day.

If a step does not work, write to support@community-access.org before the day, or come to the setup table outside the room from 10:00 on the day. Locked-down work laptop? Take the browser path: it needs nothing installed. Or write to us; we will sort it out together, and nobody is left out on the day.

Bring

  • A Windows or Mac laptop and its charger.
  • Headphones, if you use a screen reader. Read "If you use a screen reader" in the kit's README before the day; it is at letitglow.app/ahg/kit.
  • One real accessibility problem from your job. Nothing private: no student names, records or accommodation details. You will work on a sample course, and your problem is what makes your agent yours.

What the day looks like

  • Morning: design your agent in plain English, and see it work on a course document with GitHub Copilot or the assistant you chose.
  • After lunch: ground it in real evidence from GLOW, axe and Accessibility Insights, so it cites WCAG 2.2 instead of guessing.
  • Afternoon: add it to an agent team and watch the team work through the whole course, then share it to the open-source AHG 2026 collection on GitHub, with one form and your name on it.
  • The last five minutes are the session evaluation. It shapes next year's conference.

If you need anything

Large print, a particular seat, a pause, a repeat: ask for any of it, before or during. That is not an interruption of this workshop; it is the point of it.

See you in Denver,

Jeff Bishop BITS, an affiliate of the American Council of the Blind
